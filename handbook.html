01 · In the field

Fieldwork & observations

Start with the site and the sampling plan.

Page 3

Sampling and equipment

Read guidance

Good data depends on collecting samples and using equipment consistently. The handbook recommends sampling at the same part of the tidal cycle and recording the state of the tide.

Make site observations first, collect water samples for chemistry before disturbing the water, and leave activities that disturb bottom sediment until last. This helps keep one activity from affecting the next.

Prepare the equipment and forms for the measurements being taken. Follow the complete handbook and your supervisor's instructions for sample handling and instrument use.

Page 6

Site observations and data forms

Read guidance

Record the conditions at each site as well as measurements: weather, tide, water surface conditions and anything unusual can help explain changes in results.

Use the site record form to keep observations together with the site and sampling date. The full handbook includes the forms and further guidance.

02 · Water quality

Explore the measurements

Each measurement adds a different piece to the picture.

Page 13

Water temperature

Read guidance

Temperature affects aquatic organisms, chemical processes and the amount of oxygen water can hold. It varies with factors including season, time of day and tidal influence.

The handbook recommends measuring temperature as part of water-quality monitoring because it helps interpret other measurements.

Page 14

pH

Read guidance

pH describes how acidic or alkaline the water is. Monitoring it alongside other conditions helps build a picture of water quality.

Use the handbook and the instrument instructions for the chosen measurement method and recording the result.

Page 15

Water clarity

Read guidance

Suspended particles make water cloudier and reduce the depth that light can reach. Clarity can change after flooding and can be affected by erosion or algal growth.

The handbook describes tools including a Secchi disk, turbidity meter and turbidity tube. Take clarity measurements before activities that disturb sediment.

Page 16

Salinity and conductivity

Read guidance

Salinity describes dissolved salts in water. It varies across the harbour and affects aquatic organisms and how water layers form.

The handbook discusses salinity and electrical conductivity; use the approved instrument method and record the correct units and adjustments.

Page 17

Dissolved oxygen

Read guidance

Dissolved oxygen is available to aquatic plants and animals. Levels can change with temperature, salinity and time of day.

Record the result with its unit: concentration and percentage saturation are different measurements. Consult the handbook for the sampling and measurement method.

03 · Nutrients & biology

Look closer

Investigate nutrients, algae and bacterial indicators.

Page 19

Chlorophyll-a

Read guidance

Chlorophyll-a is a pigment used to estimate phytoplankton biomass. The handbook describes collecting and filtering a water sample and preparing it for laboratory analysis.

Use the full method for sample handling, storage and laboratory work.

Page 22

Phosphate (DRP)

Read guidance

Dissolved reactive phosphate is a measure of available phosphorus, a nutrient that algae can use.

Some test kits contain concentrated chemicals. Follow the kit and handbook safety instructions, including protective equipment and appropriate disposal.

Page 25

Nitrates and nitrites

Read guidance

Nitrate and nitrite measurements help describe nitrogen nutrients in the water and can contribute to understanding nutrient changes.

Use the full handbook and approved laboratory or kit instructions for collecting and analysing samples.

Page 30

Enterococci

Read guidance

Enterococci are indicators of possible faecal contamination; their presence can indicate that harmful organisms may also be present.

Follow the full method and supervisor's safety instructions for handling cultures and disposal. Cultured bacteria must remain in their sealed incubation container.
