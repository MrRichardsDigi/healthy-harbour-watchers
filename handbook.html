01 · In the field

Fieldwork & observations

Start with the site and the sampling plan.

Pages 2 & 5

Sampling order and bottles

Read guidance

Use the same site and sampling plan each visit. Note the conditions, collect clean-water samples before probes and sediment-disturbing work, then take instrument readings and clarity measurements.

  1. Note weather, tide, water-surface conditions and anything unusual at the site.
  2. Collect chemistry water samples before putting probes in the water or disturbing the site.
  3. Take instrument readings and clarity measurements next; leave activities that disturb bottom sediment until last.

Use the bottle, label, sample position and preservation instructions specified by the current lab method. Do not guess if an instruction is missing.

Page 4

Site observations

Read guidance

Record weather, tide, water-surface conditions and anything unusual. Keep notes brief, factual and free of personal information.

Enter observations with the sample in Field entry. Saved samples stay on this device until you submit them when connected.

Field instrument guide

YSI field meters

Use the correct steps for the meter and probe you have.

Page 7

Using the YSI Pro2030 and ProSolo

Read guidance

The YSI Pro2030 measures dissolved oxygen, conductivity and temperature. ProSolo readings depend on the probe fitted; an ODO/CT probe measures optical dissolved oxygen, conductivity and temperature. Check the probe label and its manual before assuming a channel is available.

  1. Check the meter and sensor. Confirm the correct probe is connected, inspect the cable and sensor for damage or fouling, and check the battery and displayed units. Ask your supervisor about any fault.
  2. Check calibration. Calibrate when required by the approved field procedure. The Pro2030 and ProSolo have different sensor and calibration steps; use the manual for the exact meter and probe. Do not collect data if calibration fails or the reading will not stabilize.
  3. Collect samples first. Take chemistry bottles before immersing the probe, then place the sensor in representative water without touching the bottom or trapping bubbles around it.
  4. Wait for stable readings. The Pro2030 dissolved-oxygen sensor needs water movement while measuring; follow its manual for the required movement. The ProSolo optical dissolved-oxygen sensor does not require stirring. Do not apply one model's procedure to the other.
  5. Record what the meter displays. Enter dissolved oxygen concentration in mg/L in the mg/L field and percent saturation in the separate % field; do not convert or swap them. Confirm the meter displays temperature in °C to match the field form. Enter conductivity or salinity only when the fitted probe and approved method provide those readings, using the method's prescribed units. Ask your supervisor if a unit is unclear.
  6. Finish safely. Rinse and store the meter and probe as instructed in that model's manual. Tell your supervisor about unstable readings, calibration errors or damaged sensors.

Calibration, sensor preparation and storage differ by model and probe. Follow the approved group procedure and the matching manufacturer's manual; this quick guide does not replace either one.

02 · Water quality

Explore the measurements

Each measurement adds a different piece to the picture.

Page 9

Water temperature

Read guidance

Temperature affects aquatic life and the amount of oxygen water can hold. It varies with season, time of day, depth and tidal influence.

Measure representative water, wait for a stable value and record the displayed temperature in °C.

Page 9

pH

Read guidance

pH describes how acidic or alkaline the water is. Use a calibrated instrument and the approved method; pH may be added later from the lab.

Page 8

Water clarity

Read guidance

Suspended particles reduce how far light reaches through water. Use a Secchi disk or calibrated turbidity meter according to the plan.

Take clarity readings before any activity that disturbs bottom sediment. Secchi depth and turbidity are different measurements.

Page 9

Salinity and conductivity

Read guidance

Salinity describes dissolved salts in water. Use the fitted probe and approved method; record the adjusted reading and units requested by the form.

Page 7

Dissolved oxygen

Read guidance

mg/L is oxygen concentration; % is percentage saturation. They are different measurements. Record the displayed values in their matching fields and do not convert between them.

Temperature, salinity, tide and time of day affect oxygen. Use current local guidance and site context when interpreting results.
